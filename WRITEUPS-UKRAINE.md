## CONFLICT: Russia-Ukraine war

Russia invaded Ukraine on 24 February 2022, eight years after seizing Crimea
and backing separatist forces in the Donbas. The drive on Kyiv failed within
weeks, and the war became a grinding contest along a front running through the
east and south, with both sides striking deep into the other's territory.
Russia holds roughly a fifth of Ukraine and the fighting continues.

---

## Kharkiv

Kharkiv is Ukraine's second city and sits thirty kilometres from the Russian
border. Russian troops reached its outskirts in February 2022 and shelled it
for months before Ukraine's September counteroffensive drove them out of the
oblast; a second Russian push north of the city opened in May 2024 and stalled
at Vovchansk. Its proximity to the border leaves it under near-daily glide bomb
and drone attack.

## Pokrovsk

Pokrovsk was the road and rail hub supplying Ukrainian positions across the
Donetsk front. Russian forces began pushing towards it in mid-2024, ground
through the approaches for over a year, and claimed the city on 2 December
2025. Russian momentum stalled after its capture, and fighting has continued in
the sectors east and north of the town.

## Donetsk

Donetsk was seized by Russian-backed forces in April 2014 and became the
capital of the self-declared Donetsk People's Republic, which Russia annexed in
September 2022. The city has been under Russian control for over a decade and
now sits well behind the front line rather than on it. It is hit by Ukrainian
shelling and drone strikes aimed at the military infrastructure inside it.

## Mariupol

Mariupol was besieged from the opening days of the invasion and destroyed in
the taking. Russian aircraft bombed the drama theatre sheltering civilians on
16 March 2022, and the last defenders surrendered at the Azovstal steelworks on
20 May; estimates of the civilian dead run into the thousands. The city has
been occupied since, and its port is now a Ukrainian drone target.

## Zaporizhzhia

Zaporizhzhia city has never been taken, but the front line has sat some forty
kilometres to its south since 2022, and Europe's largest nuclear plant — held
by Russian forces at Enerhodar downriver since March 2022 — lies inside the
same oblast. Ukraine's 2023 counteroffensive on this axis gained little ground.
The city is struck regularly, including in a wave of attacks across Ukraine on
11 August 2026.

## Kherson

Kherson was the only regional capital Russia captured intact, falling on 2
March 2022 and held for eight months before Russian forces withdrew across the
Dnipro on 11 November. The river now forms the front line and the city sits
directly on it. It is shelled daily from the occupied left bank and has become
known for drone attacks flown deliberately at civilians in the streets.

## Odesa

Odesa is Ukraine's main port and the outlet for its grain exports, which makes
its harbour infrastructure a standing target. Russian forces never reached the
city, and the naval threat receded after Ukraine pushed the Black Sea Fleet out
of the western Black Sea. Strikes now fall on port facilities and the power
grid; an attack on 9 August 2026 left the city in darkness.

## Sumy

Sumy sits on the Russian border in Ukraine's north-east and was partly overrun
in the opening weeks of the invasion before Russian forces withdrew in April
2022. It served as the staging ground for Ukraine's incursion into Kursk oblast
in August 2024, and drew a Russian counter-push into the northern part of the
oblast after that operation ended. The city is within artillery and drone range
and is struck routinely.

## Dnipro

Dnipro is the logistics and medical hub behind the eastern front, the city
through which wounded soldiers and supplies move. It has never been threatened
on the ground but is struck from the air; a missile hit an apartment block on
14 January 2023, killing forty-six people. Russian forces crossed into the
surrounding oblast in 2025 and were pushed back out in a Ukrainian
counteroffensive completed in August 2026.

## Belgorod

Belgorod is the Russian city closest to the border, forty kilometres from
Ukraine, and was a staging area for the invasion in 2022. It has been under
Ukrainian shelling and drone attack since, which makes it the Russian city most
exposed to the war. A drone attack on 9 August 2026 killed five people.

## Kursk

Kursk oblast was the target of Ukraine's only sustained offensive onto Russian
soil, launched on 6 August 2024, which seized the town of Sudzha and around a
thousand square kilometres of territory. The ground proved impossible to hold.
Russian forces, reinforced by North Korean troops, retook Sudzha in March 2025
and the campaign ended after 218 days.

## Moscow

Moscow lies some 450 kilometres from Ukraine and was first reached by Ukrainian
drones in 2023. The attacks have grown in scale since: Ukraine launched around
six hundred drones towards the city in a single night in July 2026, and strikes
have set the Moscow refinery alight more than once. They cause limited physical
damage but repeatedly close the capital's airports.

## Novorossiysk

Novorossiysk is Russia's largest Black Sea port and became the main base for
the Black Sea Fleet after Ukrainian strikes made Sevastopol untenable. It
handles a large share of Russian oil and grain exports, which makes it a target
of strategic rather than tactical value. A Ukrainian strike on the night of 11
August 2026 hit the naval base, oil terminals and grain facilities, and
suspended operations at two major grain terminals.
