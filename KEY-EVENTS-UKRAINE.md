# Key events — Ukraine

Criterion (agreed 30 Aug 2026): an event earns a place if it CHANGED THE
STATUS of the location — captured, liberated, besieged, encircled,
evacuated — or is historically significant in its own right regardless of
military effect: mass civilian casualties, a documented war crime, a
strike on a protected site. Routine strikes do not qualify however large.

Every date below was verified against a source this session. Items marked
[CONTESTED] have two defensible dates or a toll that changed between
first and final reporting — the note says which. Items marked [DECIDE]
are editorial calls that are yours, not mine.

Format: YYYY-MM-DD — one sentence, past tense, no hedging.

## Kyiv
2022-02-24 — Russian troops crossed from Belarus and an airborne assault seized Hostomel airport north-west of the city.
2022-04-02 — Russian forces completed their withdrawal from Kyiv oblast, leaving mass killings of civilians documented at Bucha and Irpin.
2022-10-10 — Russia began mass missile strikes on Ukrainian cities two days after the Kerch bridge attack, hitting central Kyiv in the morning rush hour.
2022-10-17 — Shahed drones killed four people in a residential block in Shevchenkivskyi district, among them a woman six months pregnant.
2024-07-08 — A cruise missile struck the Okhmatdyt children's hospital, killing two adults and injuring sixteen.

## Kharkiv
2022-02-24 — Russian forces reached the city's outskirts and sustained shelling of residential districts began.
2022-03-01 — Two missiles struck the regional administration building on Freedom Square, killing 29. [CONTESTED toll: same-day reports said 6–10; 29 is the April 2022 confirmed figure]
2022-09-06 — Ukraine's counteroffensive broke the front; Izium was retaken on 10 September and Russian forces were driven from most of the oblast.
2024-05-10 — Russia opened a second offensive into northern Kharkiv oblast, which stalled at Vovchansk.
2024-05-25 — A glide bomb hit the Epicentr hardware store, killing 19 and wounding more than 50.

## Pokrovsk
2023-08-07 — Two Iskander missiles hit a hotel and an apartment block forty minutes apart, the second killing rescuers who had arrived after the first; 10 died and 82 were wounded.
2024-08-19 — Ukraine ordered the mandatory evacuation of families with children as Russian forces closed on the town.
2025-12-02 — Russia claimed the capture of the city after more than a year of fighting on its approaches.

## Donetsk
2014-04-07 — Separatists occupying the regional administration building declared the Donetsk People's Republic.
2014-05-26 — Fighting for Donetsk airport began; the terminal held out until separatist forces took it on 21 January 2015.
2022-09-30 — Russia signed treaties annexing Donetsk oblast along with Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson.

## Mariupol
2022-03-02 — The city was fully encircled, cutting water, power and heat.
2022-03-09 — Russian aircraft bombed maternity hospital No. 3.
2022-03-16 — The drama theatre, sheltering civilians with "CHILDREN" written outside it, was bombed. [CONTESTED toll: Amnesty says at least 12 and likely many more; an AP investigation estimated around 600]
2022-05-20 — The last defenders at the Azovstal steelworks surrendered and Russia declared full control.

## Zaporizhzhia
2022-03-04 — Russian forces seized the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant at Enerhodar, the first time an operating nuclear station had been taken in war.
2022-09-30 — Sixteen missiles hit a civilian convoy on the city's outskirts, killing 32, hours before Russia's annexation ceremony. [CONTESTED toll: same-day reports 23–30; 32 is the confirmed figure]
2023-06-04 — Ukraine's southern counteroffensive opened on this axis and gained little ground over the summer. [CONTESTED date: ISW dates the onset to 4 June; most Western outlets reported the visible Zaporizhzhia push from 8 June]

## Kherson
2022-03-02 — Kherson fell, the only Ukrainian regional capital captured in the invasion.
2022-09-30 — Russia annexed the oblast.
2022-11-11 — Ukrainian forces entered the city as Russian troops withdrew across the Dnipro.
2023-06-06 — The Kakhovka dam was breached, flooding the lower Dnipro and affecting some 40,000 people.

## Odesa
2014-05-02 — Street clashes between rival demonstrators ended in a fire at the Trade Unions House; 42 died in the building and six in the earlier fighting. [DECIDE — see note below]
2022-07-22 — The Black Sea Grain Initiative was signed in Istanbul, reopening the port to grain exports.
2023-07-17 — Russia withdrew from the grain deal and strikes on port infrastructure followed.
2023-07-23 — Missile strikes damaged the Transfiguration Cathedral, destroying its central altar.

## Sumy
2022-02-24 — Russian columns crossed the border and parts of the oblast were occupied within days.
2022-04-08 — The governor declared the oblast clear of Russian troops. [CONTESTED: he said no settlements remained occupied on 4 April; 8 April is the declared completion]
2024-08-06 — Ukraine launched its incursion into Kursk oblast from Sumy.
2025-04-13 — Two ballistic missiles struck the city centre on Palm Sunday, killing 35.

## Dnipro
2023-01-14 — A Kh-22 missile destroyed part of a nine-storey apartment block, killing 46 including six children.
2024-11-21 — Russia used the Oreshnik intermediate-range missile in combat for the first time, against the Pivdenmash plant.
2026-08-14 — A Ukrainian counteroffensive completed the recovery of occupied ground in Dnipropetrovsk oblast.

## Belgorod
2022-04-01 — Two helicopters struck a fuel depot in the city in the first widely reported air strike on Russian soil; Ukraine did not claim responsibility and attribution remains disputed.
2023-05-22 — Fighters of the Russian Volunteer Corps and the Freedom of Russia Legion crossed into the oblast at Hraivoron.
2023-12-30 — Shelling of the city centre killed 25 including five children, the deadliest attack on a Russian city of the war.

## Kursk
2024-08-06 — Ukrainian forces crossed the border in the first sustained incursion by a foreign army into Russia since the Second World War.
2024-08-15 — Zelensky confirmed the capture of Sudzha. [CONTESTED: some accounts date effective control to 8–12 August]
2024-10-28 — NATO and the Pentagon confirmed the deployment of North Korean troops to Kursk oblast.
2025-03-16 — Ukrainian forces withdrew from Sudzha and the campaign ended after 218 days. [CONTESTED: ISW and DeepState geolocated the withdrawal to 12 March; 16 March is Ukraine's General Staff confirmation]

## Moscow
2023-05-03 — Two drones were destroyed over the Kremlin.
2023-05-30 — Drones struck residential districts, the first attack on the capital's housing.
2026-07-28 — Around six hundred drones were launched towards the city in a single night.

## Novorossiysk
2023-08-04 — Naval drones holed the landing ship Olenegorsky Gornyak in the harbour.
2023-10-01 — The Black Sea Fleet dispersed vessels from Sevastopol and Novorossiysk became its main working port. [note: a dispersal under fire, not a formal change of base]
2026-08-11 — A large strike hit the naval base, oil terminals and grain facilities, suspending operations at two grain terminals.

# Editorial notes — your call, not mine

## Odesa, 2 May 2014 [DECIDE]
The Trade Unions House fire is heavily contested and is a fixture of Russian
justification narratives for the war. Leaving it out looks like avoidance;
putting it in without care looks like endorsement of that framing. I have
worded it as neutrally as the facts allow — clashes between rival
demonstrators, the fire, the toll, no attribution of blame. Keep, cut or
reword. You know this ground better than I do.

## Crocus City Hall, 22 March 2024 — LEFT OUT
145 killed near Moscow (cumulative toll now 151). Islamic State claimed it
and Western intelligence assessed ISIS-K acting alone; Russia alleged a
Ukrainian link, which Ukraine denied and which Russia's own Investigative
Committee reportedly did not substantiate. I have left it off the Moscow
list because putting it on a Russia-Ukraine war map implies a connection to
this conflict that the evidence does not support. Tell me if you disagree —
it is the single largest loss of civilian life in Russia in this period and
there is a case for including it precisely to state what the evidence says.

## What I could not double-source

were blocked to me: the Belgorod fuel depot strike, the Novorossiysk naval
drone attack, and the Odesa cathedral damage. Dates are consistent across

## Nord Stream
2022-09-26 — Underwater explosions ruptured both Nord Stream pipelines near the Danish island of Bornholm, ending Russian pipeline gas to Germany.
2023-06-13 — Reporting revealed that Dutch intelligence had warned the CIA in June 2022 of a Ukrainian plan to attack the pipelines, and that the CIA had told Ukraine not to proceed.
2024-08-14 — German prosecutors issued an arrest warrant for a Ukrainian diver over the sabotage.
2026-07-02 — German prosecutors charged a former Ukrainian officer with directing an attack against civilian objects, alleging the operation was ordered from Ukraine.
2026-08-23 — Zelensky said Ukraine was not behind the sabotage and offered to assist the investigation.

Note: the German case is live and the accused denies the charge. Every line
above is worded as allegation where it is allegation. Do not soften this.

Seymour Hersh's February 2023 claim that US Navy divers destroyed the
pipelines is deliberately NOT listed here. It is a claim about an event, not
an event: one anonymous source, denied by the US, the CIA and Norway,
uncorroborated in the three years since, and contradicted by the German
investigation. If we build the disputedClaims field, it belongs there.
