# Gaza write-ups — first draft

Same style as Ukraine and Sudan: the place's role in the war, chronological
with hard dates, consequences named plainly, closing on the present.

Current as of 6 September 2026.

## CONFLICT: Gaza war

The war began on 7 October 2023, when Hamas-led fighters attacked southern Israel, killing around 1,200 people and taking about 250 hostage. Israel's campaign in response had killed more than 67,000 Palestinians by the time a ceasefire took effect on 10 October 2025, according to Gaza's health ministry. The ceasefire ended the full-scale war but not the dying, and Israel did not withdraw to its pre-war positions: it holds the majority of the Strip behind a demarcation called the Yellow Line.

---

## Gaza City

Gaza City was the main objective of Israel's ground invasion, which began on 27 October 2023 and encircled the city within weeks. Israeli forces raided al-Shifa, the territory's largest hospital, on 15 November 2023 and again on 18 March 2024, leaving it around 95 per cent destroyed by the WHO's assessment. The city sits on the Palestinian side of the Yellow Line, though eastern districts including al-Shujaiya were brought inside the Israeli-controlled zone in March 2026.

## Khan Younis

Khan Younis became the focus of the campaign once Gaza City had fallen, with Israeli operations opening on 1 December 2023 and a formal siege running from late January 2024. Israeli forces withdrew on 7 April 2024 but returned repeatedly over the following months, so the withdrawal marked a phase rather than an ending. Much of the city now lies behind the Yellow Line, and it is where Palestinians returning through Rafah are taken.

## Rafah

Rafah sits on the Egyptian border and became the last refuge of the war, sheltering more than 1.4 million displaced people by early 2024 and briefly becoming the most populous city in the Palestinian territories. Israel launched its offensive there on 6 May 2024 and took the border crossing the next day, closing the only route out of Gaza that Israel does not otherwise control. The crossing reopened on 2 February 2026, on foot, for roughly fifty people a day.

## Jabalia

Jabalia is the largest of Gaza's refugee camps, built for those displaced in 1948, and among the most densely populated places anywhere. Israeli airstrikes hit it on 31 October and 1 November 2023; Amnesty International found no evidence of a military objective in the first and called it a likely war crime. The camp has been fought over repeatedly since and lies close to the Yellow Line.
